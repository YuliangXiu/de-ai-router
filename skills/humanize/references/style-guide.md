# Style guide: how to write like a human

Removing AI patterns is half the job. The other half is writing with a person behind it. Adapted from brandonwise/humanizer's style guide and blader/humanizer's personality guidance.

Scope note: this applies where voice belongs (essays, posts, opinion, personal writing). For reference docs, legal text, and encyclopedic content, neutral and plain *is* the human voice.

## The read-aloud test

Read the text aloud. If it sounds like a press release, a Wikipedia article, or a customer service chatbot, it needs work. Good writing sounds like a specific person thinking on paper.

## Have opinions

Don't just report facts. React to them.

**Soulless:** "The experiment produced interesting results. Some developers were impressed while others were skeptical."

**Human:** "I don't know how to feel about this one. Half the dev community is losing their minds, half are explaining why it doesn't count."

## Vary your rhythm

Short sentences. Then longer ones that take their time getting where they're going, maybe with a clause or two along the way.

**Monotone:** "The tool works well. It processes data quickly. Users report satisfaction. The interface is clean."

**Varied:** "The tool works well. It's fast, embarrassingly fast compared to the manual process we'd been limping along with, and users seem happy, though I suspect half of them haven't found the advanced features yet."

## Acknowledge complexity

Real humans have mixed feelings. "Impressive but kind of unsettling" is more honest than pure praise or pure criticism, and models almost never write it unprompted.

## Use first person when it fits

"I" isn't unprofessional. "I've seen this go wrong when..." and "What I don't understand is..." are honest and specific.

## Let some mess in

Perfect structure feels algorithmic. Tangents, asides, parentheticals, and self-corrections signal a real person. Not every piece needs them.

## Be specific

Replace vague adjectives with concrete details:

- "Important city" -> "Portland, population 650,000"
- "Recent study" -> "A 2024 Stanford study of 500 developers"
- "Significant growth" -> "Revenue grew 34% in Q3"

The specifics must come from the source or the author. Never invent them to sound concrete; that trades one kind of slop for a worse one.

## Use simple verbs

| Instead of | Use |
|------------|-----|
| serves as | is |
| features / boasts | has |
| utilizes / leverages | uses |
| facilitates | helps |
| encompasses | includes |
| garners | gets |

## One qualifier is enough

"Could potentially possibly" means "could." Pick one qualifier and commit.

## End with something real

Don't reach for a platitude. End with a fact, a question, or nothing.

**Generic:** "The future looks bright. Exciting times lie ahead."

**Specific:** "The company plans to open two more locations next year. Whether that's ambitious or reckless depends on the Q4 numbers."

**Or just stop:** "The company plans to open two more locations next year."

## Formatting

- Sentence case for headings.
- No mechanical bold emphasis in body text.
- No emojis decorating lists or headings.
- Straight quotes.
- Commas, periods, colons, and parentheses instead of em dashes.
- No inline-header lists ("**Topic:** description of topic").
